Additional evidence-to-claim contract for this scientific-claim assessment:

Evaluate the claim as written, rather than silently replacing it with an easier, narrower claim. Align the material entity, intervention, outcome, population/location, quantity, and time horizon with the supplied evidence. Do not invent an "all", "always", lifetime horizon, or requirement for a particular experiment when the claim does not specify one. If a material ambiguity changes the verdict, state that ambiguity instead of choosing a convenient interpretation without saying so.

SUPPORT means the supplied evidence establishes the claim within its stated scope. REFUTE requires evidence contradicting that same scope; lack of evidence is not refutation. Use INSUFFICIENT when a material condition is missing or the full verdict remains unresolved. You may still state a narrower supported or contradicted finding without presenting it as a verdict on a stronger full claim.

Reasonable text-grounded inference is allowed: a direct statement in a review can be sufficient, and a new randomized trial or biochemical assay is not always required. Distinguish such inference from an extra unestablished premise. In particular, do not attribute a combined intervention's effect to one component, infer the effect of deficiency merely by reversing supplementation, turn a finite follow-up into unlimited future risk, equate different endpoints, or move a cell from one tissue to another merely because it affects that tissue. Preserve model-versus-measurement and trend-versus-established-effect qualifications.

Return a concise, auditable answer with these sections:
Decision: SUPPORT, REFUTE, or INSUFFICIENT for the claim as written. Give one unambiguous top-level decision, not two incompatible headlines.
Scope: the interpretation and scope actually established; name any material unresolved condition.
Evidence: a small number of necessary factual statements with inline [n] source references. State whether an item is a reported result or a limited inference. Cite only sources that support the corresponding statement; do not list unrelated sources simply to use all context entries.
Limits: missing evidence or important qualifications, if any. The limitations must agree with the top-level decision; a caveat is not permission to affirm a full claim that your own answer says is not established.

Use only the supplied context. Omit background facts that the context does not establish, even if they seem familiar. Do not fabricate quotations, measurements, or study details. Favor about 120-220 words when sufficient, but do not omit a material limitation or needed evidence just to meet this length preference. Provide the decision and concise supporting evidence, not a step-by-step internal reasoning transcript.
